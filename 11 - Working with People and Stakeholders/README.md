# 11 - Working with People and Stakeholders

> **Core Principle:** *"Six different audiences, six different slide decks. The art of product management is not saying the same thing to everyone — it's saying the right thing, at the right level of detail, to the right person."*

---

## General Communication Skills

**The problem it solves:** A product manager is a communication hub — connecting engineers, designers, executives, sales, marketing, legal, and customers. Each group needs different information at different levels of detail.

### Communication Channels

| Channel | Key Principle |
|---------|---------------|
| **Meetings** | Face-to-face time is the highest-leverage communication. Send recaps and follow-up notes afterward. |
| **Conference calls / Video** | Intonation and clarity matter more when body language is limited. Share resources in advance. |
| **Email** | Primary method. Be clear, concise, include links to referenced data and resources. |
| **Informal (lunch, hallway)** | Underestimated. Great for relationships, honest feedback, building support for your ideas. |

**The principle:** *Communication is not about transmitting information. It's about ensuring the right person has the right context to make the right decision.*

---

## Working with Engineers

### What Engineers Need: Detail
Engineers think in systems. Vague descriptions lead to incorrect implementations, rework, and frustration.

**Engineers need to know:** Error states, empty states, edge cases, internationalization behavior.

### The Tips

| # | Tip | Why It Matters |
|---|-----|----------------|
| 1 | **If something goes wrong, it's your fault.** | You own the specs. Wrong specs = your responsibility. |
| 2 | **Share the future vision.** | Helps engineers make better technical architecture decisions now. |
| 3 | **Do the work upfront.** | Engineers' time is expensive. Don't waste it on unclear requirements. |
| 4 | **Be cognizant of tech debt.** | Engineers deal with it later. Trust their judgment on build quality. |
| 5 | **Don't treat engineers like an agency.** | Involve them in product discussions. They have great ideas beyond coding. |

## Working with Designers

### What Designers Need: Problems, Not Solutions
Designers are creative experts in user interaction. Telling them *how* to design is as bad as telling engineers *how* to code. Give them the problem and let them explore solutions.

### The Tips

| # | Tip | Why It Matters |
|---|-----|----------------|
| 1 | **Be clear about constraints upfront.** | One constraint can change an entire design. Tell them early. |
| 2 | **Don't treat designers as an agency.** | They know more about user interactions than you do. Involve them in product decisions, not just visual polish. |
| 3 | **Function as a team.** | Show them user research, data, and feedback. Give them context, not commands. |
| 4 | **Don't tell the designer what to do.** | You own the *what* and *why*, not the *how*. Describe problems in broad terms. Let them find the solution. |
| 5 | **Talk about user problems first, solutions second.** | Describe the problem ("users can't express themselves with photos") not the solution ("users should be able to send photos"). Designers find better solutions when given the problem. |

**The principle:** *Your job is to define the problem and constraints. The designer's job is to explore the solution space. Don't rob them of the creative work.*

## Working with Executives and Other Stakeholders

### The Communication Shift
Engineers need detail. Executives need the opposite — **brevity and business impact.**

| Audience | What They Want | How to Communicate |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Executives** | Brief updates, business impact | 2 bullet points, not 2 paragraphs. Speak in revenue, metrics, and strategic effect. |
| **Sales** | How the product helps them sell | Business outcomes, competitive positioning, timeline |
| **Marketing** | What's launching and when | Launch narratives, customer stories, key features |
| **Legal** | Compliance, risk, regulatory | Data handling, privacy, terms details |

### Tips for All Stakeholders

| # | Tip |
|---|-----|
| 1 | Talk to people on each team as much as possible. Relationships matter. |
| 2 | Demonstrate you understand the user, technology, and business. Trust in your decisions comes from trust in your competence. |
| 3 | Update frequently. Be clear whether you're asking for a response or just informing. |
| 4 | When announcing completed work, tell the *story* — what options were considered, why this solution won. |

### The Executive Principle
The instructor: *"Executives are the busiest people at the company. Sending them compact but meaningful messages shows that you value their time."*

> *"Write two bullet points, not two paragraphs. If they want more detail, they know who to ask — you."*

## The Meta-Principle: Audience Adaptation

The instructor opens with: *"Six different audiences, six different slide decks."*

This is not dishonesty. It's **respecting that each audience has different decision-making needs.** The skill is selecting the right information for the right person at the right time.

| If you communicate to... | Like this... | You fail because... |
|--------------------------|-------------|-------------------|
| Engineers like executives | Brief, no detail | They can't build from that |
| Executives like engineers | Full detail, long email | They won't read it |
| Designers like order-takers | "Make this button blue" | You waste their expertise |
| Anyone with no context | "Just build it" | They make wrong decisions |

**The principle:** *Adapt your communication to the audience's needs, not your own comfort. Great product managers don't say everything — they say what matters to the person they're talking to.*
**The principle:** *Engineers are your partners, not order-takers. Treat them as co-owners of the product.*
